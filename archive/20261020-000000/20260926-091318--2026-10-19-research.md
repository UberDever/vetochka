## Contextual equivalence

- Evidence (agent): Pitts and Stark define contextual equivalence through agreement of observations in every admissible program context. Their model includes functions and local state. See Definition 2.2 and the memoisation examples.
- Interpretation (agent): this is a precise existing candidate for the user's indistinguishability intuition. It does not establish metaphysical identity or provide an executable equality test.
- Source: Andrew Pitts and Ian Stark, *Operational Reasoning for Functions with Local State*. https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/stark/operfl.pdf

## Bisimulation and hidden state

- Evidence (agent): Sangiorgi, Kobayashi, and Sumii develop environmental bisimulations for higher-order languages, including a calculus with higher-order store. They prove correspondence with contextual equivalence for their calculi.
- Evidence (agent): their environment records the observer's knowledge. It is not a requirement to expose runtime closure environments.
- Interpretation (agent): this supplies a proof approach for ongoing interactions without enumerating a complete behavioral profile. Vetochka's reflective observations would require additional treatment.
- Source: *Environmental Bisimulations for Higher-Order Languages*, LICS 2007, introduction and Sections 3–4. https://www-kb.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~koba/papers/lics2007.pdf

## I/O has a structural semantic account

- Evidence (agent): Plotkin and Pretnar describe algebraic effects through operations and equations. Their I/O example uses read and write operations with continuations determined by the outcome.
- Interpretation (agent): operation identity, arguments, outcomes, and subsequent behavior can describe an I/O interface without exposing hardware implementation.
- Proposal (agent): use this account to test the ontology. No decision to add effect handlers to Vetochka follows.
- Source: *Handling Algebraic Effects*, introduction and Section 2.1. https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/gdp/publications/handling-algebraic-effects.pdf
